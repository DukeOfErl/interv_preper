# Expected competencies by seniority tier

Use these anchors to calibrate questions and scoring to the level the
candidate is interviewing for — a strong junior answer can be a weak senior
answer to the same question.

## Junior (0–2 years)

- Writes working, readable code with guidance; asks good questions rather
  than guessing silently.
- Debugs methodically within a component; knows when to escalate.
- Interview signal: learning velocity, fundamentals, receptiveness to
  feedback. Red flag at this level: pretending to know rather than saying
  "I don't know".

## Mid-level (2–5 years)

- Owns a feature end to end: clarifies requirements, designs within the
  existing architecture, tests, ships, and monitors it.
- Anticipates edge cases and failure modes; writes code others can extend.
- Interview signal: unprompted trade-off reasoning, first-person war stories
  with results. Red flag: every story's scope is a task someone else framed.

## Senior (5+ years)

- Designs systems spanning several components; makes and defends trade-offs
  under ambiguity; de-risks delivery with increments and reversibility.
- Multiplies others: reviews, mentors, raises the bar without being asked.
- Interview signal: chooses *not* to build things, names the second-order
  consequences of designs, stories where they changed another team's outcome.
  Red flag: depth in one stack but no judgment transfer outside it.

## Staff / principal

- Operates across teams: aligns architecture with organizational direction,
  spots the problem nobody owns, writes things down that outlive them.
- Interview signal: impact through influence rather than authority; a track
  record of picking the right battles. Red flag: seniority claimed through
  tenure and titles rather than through decisions and their outcomes.
