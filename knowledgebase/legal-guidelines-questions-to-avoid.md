# Legal guidelines — questions not to ask

> General guidance for interview practice, not legal advice. Rules differ by
> jurisdiction and change over time; when in doubt, ask only about the
> candidate's ability to perform the job.

## Broadly off-limits topics (most jurisdictions)

Do not ask about, or fish for:

- **Age or dates that reveal it** ("when did you graduate high school?").
- **Family status**: marital status, pregnancy, plans to have children,
  childcare arrangements.
- **Origin**: nationality, ethnicity, birthplace, native language, accent,
  citizenship of relatives.
- **Religion**: observance, holidays kept, sabbath availability (ask instead
  about ability to work the published schedule, uniformly).
- **Health and disability**: diagnoses, medications, past sick leave, workers'
  compensation history. Only ask whether the candidate can perform the
  essential functions of the job, with or without reasonable accommodation.
- **Sexual orientation and gender identity.**
- **Arrests** (as opposed to convictions — and conviction questions are
  themselves restricted in many places, e.g. "ban the box" laws in many US
  states limit when in the process they may be asked).

## Jurisdiction notes

- **United States**: federal law (Title VII, ADA, ADEA) prohibits questions
  targeting protected classes; salary-history questions are additionally
  banned in many states and cities.
- **United Kingdom / European Union**: the Equality Act 2010 (UK) and national
  equal-treatment laws (EU) cover similar protected characteristics;
  pre-offer health questionnaires are tightly restricted in the UK.
- **Israel**: the Employment (Equal Opportunities) Law prohibits
  discrimination by age, sex, sexual orientation, parenthood, religion,
  nationality, and military-reserve service, among others; questions about
  pregnancy or reserve duty are a frequent pitfall.

## The safe reframe

Every legitimate need behind a forbidden question has a job-related form:

- Not "do you have kids?" but "this role requires on-call one week a month —
  does the published schedule work for you?"
- Not "where is your accent from?" but "the role requires professional
  fluency in English — tell me about a project you ran in English."
- Not "how old are you?" but "which of the required technologies have you
  used in production, and for how long?"
