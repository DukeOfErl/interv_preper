# Interview best practices

## Structure the interview

- Open with a short agenda: introductions, the interview stages, and time for
  the candidate's questions at the end. Predictability reduces anxiety and
  produces better signal.
- Ask every candidate for a role the same core questions in the same order.
  Structured interviews are markedly more predictive of job performance than
  unstructured conversations, and far easier to compare across candidates.
- One question at a time. Compound questions ("tell me about a conflict and
  also how you prioritize") get half-answers to each part.

## Ask better questions

- Prefer behavioral questions about the past ("tell me about a time you…")
  over hypotheticals ("what would you do if…"). Past behavior is evidence;
  hypotheticals mostly measure eloquence.
- Probe with the STAR frame: Situation, Task, Action, Result. If any part is
  missing — most often the candidate's *individual* action or a measurable
  result — ask a follow-up rather than moving on.
- Follow the evidence, not the vibe. "We shipped it" invites "what part did
  you personally build?"; "the migration went well" invites "what went wrong
  along the way?".
- Silence is a tool. After a hard question, give the candidate time to think;
  filling the pause for them destroys the signal.

## Evaluate well

- Take notes on what the candidate *said and did*, not conclusions ("gave a
  concrete example with a measured 30% latency win", not "smart"). Score each
  competency right after the interview, before impressions fade or blur.
- Rate against the rubric for the role and level, not against the previous
  candidate or yourself.
- Calibrate on evidence quantity: a strong answer is specific, recent,
  first-person, and has a verifiable outcome. A weak answer is generic,
  team-attributed ("we"), or unfalsifiable.

## Candidate experience

- The interview is also the candidate evaluating the employer. Leave real
  time for their questions and answer them plainly.
- Explain next steps and the timeline before closing, and stick to them.
