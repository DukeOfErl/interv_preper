# Behavioral question bank (all roles)

Each question lists the competency it probes and indicators of strong and weak
answers. Probe incomplete answers with the STAR frame (Situation, Task,
Action, Result).

## Ownership & delivery

- "Tell me about a project you led end to end. What was the outcome?"
  — *probes ownership.* Strong: names their individual decisions, a concrete
  measurable result, and what they'd do differently. Weak: only "we" language,
  no result, or a result they can't quantify or verify.
- "Tell me about a time you missed a deadline or shipped something late."
  — *probes accountability.* Strong: owns the miss, explains the recovery and
  what changed afterwards. Weak: blames others or claims it never happens.

## Conflict & collaboration

- "Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or manager that mattered.
  How was it resolved?" — *probes conflict handling.* Strong: steelmans the
  other side, separates the issue from the person, describes a resolution
  process. Weak: the story is about winning, or the conflict was left to rot.
- "Describe a time you had to deliver hard feedback, or received some."
  — *probes directness with care.* Strong: specific, timely, private feedback
  with follow-up. Weak: avoided it, or recounts feedback given harshly.

## Adaptability & learning

- "Tell me about a time priorities changed abruptly under you."
  — *probes adaptability.* Strong: re-planned explicitly, communicated
  trade-offs to stakeholders. Weak: soldiered on with the stale plan or
  quietly dropped commitments.
- "What is something significant you learned in the last year, and how?"
  — *probes growth.* Strong: concrete skill, deliberate practice, applied
  since. Weak: can't name anything specific.

## Judgment

- "Tell me about the hardest trade-off you had to make recently."
  — *probes decision-making.* Strong: names the options, the criteria, who
  was consulted, and revisits whether it proved right. Weak: presents the
  decision as obvious or can't reconstruct the reasoning.
- "Tell me about a time you were wrong about something important."
  — *probes intellectual honesty.* Strong: specific error, how they noticed,
  what they changed. Weak: a humble-brag ("too much of a perfectionist").
